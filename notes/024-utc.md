# 024: UTC, the worlds reference clock
builds on: [001 data is facts written down](./001-data-is-facts-and-numbers.md), [023 true and false, the boolean](./023-true-and-false-the-boolean.md)
section: data at the atom level, part 24, ~2 min read

a boolean is one fact. the next kind of fact is a moment. order 101 in the shop, the imaginary gujarati snack store these notes run on, says ordered_at 2026-04-01 09:15:00, a date and a time. nine fifteen where? the column never says.

UTC, Coordinated Universal Time, is one clock the whole world agrees to measure against. every other clock is UTC plus or minus some hours.

| where | clock reads | compared to UTC |
|---|---|---|
| UTC | 2026-04-01 13:15 | the reference |
| New York | 2026-04-01 09:15 | 4 hours behind |
| Mumbai | 2026-04-01 18:45 | 5.5 hours ahead |

one moment, three readings, and all three are right. if a database stores just "09:15", you cant tell which clock it came from.

i expected one of the big cities to be the reference, but the reference clock belongs to no city. it isnt New York or London, and it has no daylight saving, the clock change many places make twice a year. New York is 4 behind in April but 5 behind in winter, while UTC stays put.

when a time is written in UTC it usually ends in a Z, like 2026-04-01T13:15:00Z. the T just separates date from time, and Z means no hours added or removed from UTC. thats from RFC 3339, the written internet standard for timestamps: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3339

so what: if two servers, computers running your app, write log lines (a diary of events) in Mumbai and New York time, sorting those lines by their clock text puts events in the wrong order. UTC is how every system agrees on "when" without arguing about cities. how a computer keeps that moment as one plain number is next.

### check yourself

1. order 101 was placed by someone whose clock read 09:15 in New York on 2026-04-01. what does UTC read at that moment?
<details><summary>answer</summary>

13:15. New York is 4 hours behind UTC in April, so UTC is 4 hours ahead of the New York clock, add 4. the tempting answer is 05:15, from subtracting because "behind" sounds like minus.

</details>

2. a Mumbai server logs an event at 22:30 on April 1. a New York server logs one at 20:00 on April 1. sorted by the clock text, New York looks first. which really happened first?
<details><summary>answer</summary>

Mumbai. 22:30 in Mumbai is 17:00 UTC; 20:00 in New York is 00:00 UTC on April 2. the tempting answer is New York, because 20:00 is smaller than 22:30, but those numbers come from different clocks.

</details>
